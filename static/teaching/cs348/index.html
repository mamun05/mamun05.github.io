    Syllabus · Spring 2023

    Course overview

    Introduction to fundamental concepts of database management using primarily the relational model. Schema design and refinement. Query languages. Database application development. Transactions and concurrency control. Overview of NoSQL databases, including graph and document stores. Overview of OLAP and data warehousing.

    Learning goals

    1. Design effective database schemas that accurately model real-world information semantics.
    2. Learn about the different data models (relational, graph, and document) used in different database systems.
    3. Pose complex database queries in SQL and NoSQL languages, as well as in theoretical query languages.
    4. Gain an introductory background in indexing.
    5. Learn about transactions and concurrency control.
    6. Differentiate between OLTP and OLAP applications.
    Prerequisites
    CS 25100: Data Structures and Algorithms.

    Homework

    Seven graded assignments, each with its own submission instructions. Written work was typed (LaTeX, Word, or similar) and submitted as PDF to Gradescope; code assignments such as SQL and MongoDB queries went to Brightspace.

    • Late work was accepted up to 72 hours after the due date, with a 10% penalty per day.
    • Each student had five penalty-free extension days for the semester, at most three on any one assignment, since solutions were posted 72 hours after the due date.

    Midterm exams

    • Midterm 1: Mon, Mar 6, 2023, 8:00–9:30 pm, LILY 1105
    • Midterm 2: Thu, Apr 20, 2023, 8:00–9:30 pm, LILY 1105

    Each exam covered about half of the course topics and could be open-book and/or open-note, with details announced the week before. Per university policy, one class was canceled for each evening exam. Make-up exams were given only for fully documented illnesses or other emergencies.

    Quizzes

    A weekly Brightspace quiz in most weeks. Students had three attempts and the best one counted. Questions were multiple-choice, true/false, and multi-select; multi-select was scored as right answers minus wrong answers. Quizzes were auto-graded, so grades appeared immediately.

    Hotseat questions

    Lectures included hotseat questions for students in the classroom. The lowest 25% of each student's hotseat scores were dropped to cover missed classes for medical or other reasons. Students with a DRC letter who could not answer in class contacted the instructor.

    Regrade requests

    Submitted within three days of receiving graded work: on Gradescope for Gradescope assignments, or as a private Campuswire post for Brightspace assignments. A valid request shows that the student checked the posted solution carefully and explains clearly why a regrade is warranted.

    Communication

    Brightspace

    Most class content: the syllabus, the schedule, lecture slides, and links to lecture videos. Also weekly quizzes and submission of code assignments such as SQL and MongoDB queries.

    Campuswire

    All announcements, plus questions and answers. Students could ask and answer anonymously; the instructor and TAs answered or endorsed student answers, aiming to respond within 24 hours.

    Gradescope

    Submission of PDF homework, and regrade requests for those assignments.

    Lead TAs

    Every assignment had a lead TA to contact about general issues other than regrades. Grading was shared among several TAs, all of whom could see private questions.

    Getting help, in order

    1. Discuss the question with classmates. Asking and explaining builds understanding, but solutions are never shared.
    2. Search Campuswire for a similar question before posting.
    3. Post a public question on Campuswire. Public questions are prioritized because everyone benefits from the answer.
    4. Attend instructor or TA office hours, or ask the TA in your PSO.
    5. Post a private Campuswire question when it includes your own solution, as in regrade requests. Private questions reach the instructor and all TAs, which beats emailing one person.

    Course policies

    Academic integrity

    Academic integrity is a critical foundation for any form of higher education. Students were asked to read Purdue's academic integrity guidance. Posting homework questions or solutions (or any other part of an assignment) online was not allowed.

    Disability Resource Center

    Purdue University strives to make learning experiences as accessible as possible. Students who anticipate or experience physical or academic barriers based on disability are encouraged to contact the Disability Resource Center (drc@purdue.edu, 765-494-1247), and are welcome to let the instructor know so that options can be discussed.

    Students with a DRC recommendation letter who cannot take hotseat questions in class should contact the instructor. Students with exam-time extensions schedule their midterms with the DRC testing center a few weeks before each exam.

    Emergency preparation

    In the event of a major campus emergency, course requirements, deadlines, and grading percentages are subject to changes that may be necessitated by a revised semester calendar or other circumstances beyond the instructor's control. Relevant changes would be posted on Campuswire, the course schedule, and the syllabus.

    Mental health

    • For mental health services, contact Counseling and Psychological Services (CAPS) at 765-494-6995, during and after hours, on weekends and holidays, or visit the CAPS office on the second floor of the Purdue University Student Health Center (PUSH) during business hours.
